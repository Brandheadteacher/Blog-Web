Svijet reklame — AI & marketing consulting

Umjetna inteligencija neće zamijeniti marketing. Prosječan marketing hoće

Artificial Intelligence Won't Replace Marketing. Average Marketing Will.

AI neće ubiti 4P-a. Pokazat će koliko ih je malo tvrtki ikada ozbiljno primjenjivalo.

AI won't kill the 4Ps. It will reveal how few companies ever seriously applied them in the first place.

Branimir Zoraja 26. srpnja 2026.July 26, 2026 7 min čitanja7 min read

AI neće ubiti 4P-a. Pokazat će koliko ih je malo tvrtki ikada ozbiljno primjenjivalo.

U udžbenicima marketinga sva su 4P-a marketinškog miksa ravnopravna. U stvarnom je životu jedno P obično hipertrofirano (Promotion), dva su zanemarena (Product i Place), a jedno je predano financijama ili upravi (Price).

Marketingu uglavnom ostaje Promotion. Zatim se svi čude što ga uprava doživljava kao odjel za izradu ambalaže i oglasa.

Istraživanje Marketing Weeka iz 2025. pokazalo je koliko je ta deformacija ozbiljna: 88,7% marketinških stručnjaka ima utjecaj na oglašavanje i komunikacije, ali samo 34,1% na cijene, a 32,7% na distribuciju. Drugim riječima, marketing smije govoriti, ali često ne smije odlučivati što se prodaje, po kojoj cijeni i gdje.

To nije marketinški menadžment. To je dekoriranje tuđih odluka.

Sad se pojavio AI pa su oni koji misle da mogu bez marketinga upravljati s 4P-a dobili snažan alat. Ali AI neće ubiti 4P-a. Naprotiv, pokazat će koliko ih je malo tvrtki ikada ozbiljno primjenjivalo.

Više ideja nije isto što i bolji proizvod

U prethodnom članku pokazali smo da AI može optimizirati marketing. Ali ne zna koji stvarni problem proizvod rješava, koju želju ili emociju pokreće, zašto je drukčiji od drugih i zašto pripada baš tom brendu. AI može izmisliti tisuću proizvoda. Brend menadžer mora pronaći jedan razlog zbog kojeg proizvod postoji.

Dobar brend menadžer nije tvornica ideja. On je carinik koji odlučuje koje ideje smiju prijeći granicu brenda.

Coca-Cola Y3000 Zero Sugar predstavljena je kao okus budućnosti, razvijen kombinacijom ljudskih uvida i umjetne inteligencije. AI je pomogao interpretirati kako ljudi zamišljaju budućnost kroz emocije, boje, aspiracije i okuse. Proizvod je zatim podržan digitalnim iskustvom i spektaklom u lasvegaškoj dvorani Sphere.

Pametno?

Kao ograničena serija i generator publiciteta – da. Kao temeljna inovacija proizvoda – znatno manje.

„Okus 3000. godine“ nije potreba potrošača. To je kulturna kostimografija. Coca-Cola nije riješila problem žeđi, prehrane ili funkcionalnosti. Proizvela je razlog za razgovor. To je legitimno dok god se inovacija proizvoda ne zamjenjuje PR-viješću.

AI može kombinirati podatke o okusima, trendovima i preferencijama. Može predložiti Coca-Colu s okusom nostalgije, optimizma ili postapokaliptične vanilije.

Ali brend menadžer mora postaviti neugodnije pitanje: je li to proizvod koji ljudima treba ili samo proizvod s kojim će marketinški popuniti rupu u planu novih proizvoda? Strateški nam možda ne bi trebao. Ekstenzija koja povećava prodaju, ali smanjuje jasnoću brenda, često je računovodstveni dobitak i simbolički gubitak.

Nike je pokazao model budućnosti. Dizajnerski timovi koristili su generativni AI za stvaranje stotina vizualnih mogućnosti obuće, inspiriranih pojedinim sportašima. Zatim su, Nikeovim riječima, „odložili inteligentnu olovku“ i proizvode prilagodili preciznim zahtjevima vrhunskih sportaša. To je prava podjela rada. AI je proširio prostor mogućnosti. Ljudi su ga suzili na ono što ima smisla.

AI je divergentni stroj. Brend menadžer mora biti konvergentni autoritet.

Jer više ideja nije isto što i bolji proizvod.

Einstein bi rekao: AI može izbrojiti sve što se može napraviti. Brend menadžer mora odlučiti što se računa.

AI u području proizvoda može: analizirati nezadovoljene potrebe i trendove, generirati koncepte, recepte, dizajne i prototipove, simulirati reakcije segmenata te ubrzati testiranje i razvoj.

Brend menadžer mora: odrediti koju potrebu marka ima pravo rješavati, zaštititi prepoznatljivost i vjerodostojnost, razlikovati inovaciju od noviteta i ubiti 49 od 50 prijedloga.

Algoritam zna cijenu svega, a vrijednost ničega

AI zna koliko će kupac platiti.

Amazon strojno učenje koristi za eksperimentiranje s cijenama kako bi preciznije procijenio učinke različitih cjenovnih politika (Amazon Science).

To je racionalno. Cijena je podatak, signal i poluga. Ali je i moralna poruka.

AI može izračunati da će kupac u petak navečer platiti više za hotelsku sobu. Može otkriti da će roditelj pred početak školske godine prihvatiti višu cijenu tenisica. Može predvidjeti koliko je korisnik spreman platiti prije nego što odustane.

Algoritam vidi elastičnost. Kupac vidi poštenje ili iskorištavanje.

Kupac je poput tvoje žene, rekao bi Ogilvy: možeš ju prevariti samo prvi put.

Mogli bismo reći parafrazirajući Oscara Wildea: algoritam zna cijenu svega, a vrijednost ničega.

Zamislimo da dva kupca u istoj trgovini dobiju različitu cijenu iste paste za zube jer je AI procijenio da je jedan manje osjetljiv na cijenu. Financijski direktor vidi maržu. Potrošač vidi kaznu za lojalnost.

Brend menadžer mora razumjeti da je cijena dio identiteta. Premium cijena govori o statusu, sigurnosti ili kvaliteti. Niska cijena može govoriti o demokratičnosti i dostupnosti. Stalni popust nauči kupca kako je puna cijena namijenjena samo nestrpljivima i neinformiranima. AI će takvu ovisnost još učinkovitije održavati. Izračunat će idealan popust, idealno vrijeme i idealnog kupca. Ali neće sam pitati pretvaramo li brend u kupon za popust.

AI u području cijena može: izračunavati cjenovnu elastičnost, pratiti konkurentske cijene, simulirati popuste i pakete ili preporučivati optimalne cjenovne razrede.

Brend menadžer mora: definirati vrijednosnu arhitekturu, procijeniti utjecaj cijene na percepciju, zaštititi osjećaj pravednosti i odlučiti kada ne treba uzeti svaki euro koji je kupac spreman dati.

Kanal više nije samo poruka. Kanal postaje prodavač

AI zna gdje je kupac. Brend menadžer mora znati gdje brend pripada.

Place se često prevodi kao distribucija, a zatim predaje odjelu prodaje. Kao da mjesto prodaje nema nikakve veze s pozicioniranjem. A ima gotovo sve.

Nike je 2026. najavio integraciju s Googleovim AI commerce sustavom kako bi korisnici u manje koraka mogli prijeći od pronalaženja proizvoda razgovorom s AI asistentom do kupnje.

To je budućnost distribucije: trgovina više nije nužno mjesto na koje kupac odlazi. Trgovina se pojavljuje unutar pitanja koje kupac postavlja. Kupac neće morati otvoriti internetsku trgovinu i pregledati dvadeset modela. Pitat će AI agenta: „Koje tenisice trebam za polumaraton ako imam problema s koljenima?“

Tada se bitka za Place više neće voditi samo na polici ili Googleovoj tražilici. Vodit će se unutar algoritamske preporuke.

Parafrazirajući McLuhana, mogli bismo reći: kanal više nije samo poruka. Kanal postaje prodavač.

AI u području distribucije može: predviđati lokalnu potražnju, optimizirati zalihe i dostavu, preporučivati lokacije te skraćivati put od interesa do kupnje.

Brend menadžer mora: definirati ulogu svakog kanala, odlučiti treba li marka biti posvuda, uskladiti dostupnost s pozicioniranjem te razumjeti kada praktičnost povećava vrijednost, a kada uništava poželjnost.

Promotion je tek glasnogovornik

Da parafraziramo Hamleta u AI-ju: promovirati ili ne promovirati više nije pitanje. Pitanje je imate li uopće nešto vrijedno promoviranja.

Cadburyjeva kampanja „Shah Rukh Khan – My Ad“ upotrijebila je umjetnu inteligenciju kako bi poznati glumac postao virtualni ambasador tisuća malih lokalnih trgovina. U jednoj verziji oglasa spominjala se jedna trgovina, u drugoj druga. Tehnologija nije služila kao dekoracija, nego je omogućila ideju koju klasična produkcija ne bi mogla izvesti u tom opsegu. To je dobra uporaba AI-ja. Ne zato što je oglas personaliziran. Nego zato što je personalizacija bila sastavni dio značenja: velika zvijezda posuđuje svoju slavu malim trgovcima.

Tehnologija je pojačala društvenu i komercijalnu ideju.

Nasuprot tome, proizvesti 5.000 verzija bannera u kojima se mijenjaju ime grada, boja majice i fotografija proizvoda nije nužno inovacija. To je često samo industrijalizirana beznačajnost.

Starbucks je početkom 2026. predstavio loyalty program s više razina, personalizacijom i različitim pogodnostima, dok AI koristi i za predviđanje dostupnosti proizvoda te bolju operativnu usklađenost.

Ovdje se promocija spaja s proizvodom, cijenom i distribucijom. Upravo tako i treba biti.

Parafrazirajući Sun Tzua, možemo reći: marketinška kampanja je dobivena ili izgubljena prije nego što je prvi oglas objavljen – u proizvodu, cijeni i distribuciji.

Promotion je tek glasnogovornik. Ne može izliječiti ono što su ostala tri P-a već pokvarila.

David Ogilvy razlikovao je učinkovito od tek efikasnog oglašavanja: „Efficient advertising identifies potential customers; effective advertising can create them.“ (Ogilvy).

AI će marketing učiniti efikasnijim. Brend menadžer još uvijek mora osigurati da bude učinkovit.

AI u području promocije može: stvarati velik broj varijanti, personalizirati sadržaj i ponude, optimizirati medijski zakup, testirati naslove, vizuale i formate te identificirati trenutak najveće vjerojatnosti kupnje.

Brend menadžer mora: definirati jednu središnju ideju, odlučiti što je dopušteno personalizirati, čuvati ton, memoriju i distinktivne elemente te razlikovati reakciju od poslovnog učinka.

Princip

Najvažniji zaključak nije da će AI promijeniti 4P-a. AI će ih brutalno razotkriti.

Ako proizvod nije relevantan, AI će brže razviti njegove varijacije. Ako cijena nije vjerodostojna, AI će preciznije pronaći granicu kupčeva strpljenja. Ako je distribucija loša, AI će učinkovitije oglašavati proizvod kojeg nema. Ako promocija nema ideju, AI će proizvesti više formata istog ništa.

Da parafraziramo Druckera: cilj marketinga nije samo učiniti prodaju suvišnom. Cilj je učiniti brend toliko smislenim da promocija više ne mora popravljati ostatak miksa.

Umjetna inteligencija neće spasiti loš marketinški miks. Učinit će ga bržim, jeftinijim i detaljnije mjerljivim.

AI može pomoći u svakom od četiri P-a. Ali ne može preuzeti peti P.

Princip.

A bez principa, Product postaje novitet, Price kalkulacija, Place logistika, a Promotion buka.

Zato glavni posao brend menadžera nije upravljati četirima slovima P. Njegov je posao osigurati da sva četiri znače isto.

AI won't kill the 4Ps. It will reveal how few companies ever seriously applied them in the first place.

In marketing textbooks, all four Ps of the marketing mix are equal. In real life, one P is usually hypertrophied (Promotion), two are neglected (Product and Place), and one has been handed over to finance or management (Price).

Marketing is mostly left holding Promotion. And then everyone's surprised that management sees it as the department that makes packaging and ads.

A 2025 Marketing Week survey showed just how serious this distortion is: 88.7% of marketers have influence over advertising and communications, but only 34.1% over pricing, and 32.7% over distribution. In other words, marketing is allowed to talk, but it's often not allowed to decide what gets sold, at what price, or where.

That's not marketing management. That's decorating someone else's decisions.

Now AI has arrived, and everyone who thinks they can run the 4Ps without marketing just got a powerful new tool. But AI won't kill the 4Ps. On the contrary — it will reveal how few companies ever seriously applied them in the first place.

More ideas is not the same as a better product

In the previous article, we showed that AI can optimise marketing. But it doesn't know what real problem the product solves, what desire or emotion it triggers, why it's different from the others, or why it belongs to that particular brand. AI can dream up a thousand products. The brand manager has to find the one reason the product should exist.

A good brand manager isn't an idea factory. They're the customs officer who decides which ideas are allowed to cross the brand's border.

Coca-Cola Y3000 Zero Sugar was launched as the taste of the future, developed through a combination of human insight and artificial intelligence. AI helped interpret how people imagine the future through emotions, colours, aspirations and flavours. The product was then backed by a digital experience and a spectacle at the Sphere in Las Vegas.

Smart?

As a limited edition and a publicity generator — yes. As fundamental product innovation — considerably less so.

"The taste of the year 3000" isn't a consumer need. It's cultural costume design. Coca-Cola didn't solve a problem of thirst, nutrition or functionality. It produced a reason to talk. That's legitimate — as long as product innovation doesn't get replaced by a press release.

AI can combine data on flavours, trends and preferences. It can propose a Coca-Cola that tastes like nostalgia, optimism, or post-apocalyptic vanilla.

But the brand manager has to ask the more uncomfortable question: is this a product people need, or just a product marketing will use to fill a hole in the new-product roadmap? Strategically, we might not need it at all. A line extension that boosts sales but dilutes brand clarity is often an accounting win and a symbolic loss.

Nike showed what the model of the future looks like. Design teams used generative AI to create hundreds of visual possibilities for footwear, inspired by individual athletes. Then, in Nike's own words, they "put the intelligent pencil down" and tailored the products to the precise demands of elite athletes. That's the real division of labour. AI expanded the space of possibilities. People narrowed it down to what actually made sense.

AI is a divergent machine. The brand manager has to be the convergent authority.

Because more ideas is not the same as a better product.

Einstein might put it this way: AI can count everything that can be done. The brand manager has to decide what counts.

In product, AI can: analyse unmet needs and trends; generate concepts, formulas, designs and prototypes; simulate segment reactions; and accelerate testing and development.

The brand manager has to: decide which need the brand has the right to solve; protect recognisability and credibility; tell innovation apart from novelty; and kill 49 out of every 50 proposals.

The algorithm knows the price of everything, and the value of nothing

AI knows how much the customer will pay.

Amazon uses machine learning to experiment with prices, in order to more precisely estimate the effects of different pricing policies (Amazon Science).

That's rational. Price is data, a signal, and a lever. But it's also a moral message.

AI can calculate that a customer will pay more for a hotel room on a Friday night. It can discover that a parent will accept a higher price for sneakers right before the school year starts. It can predict exactly how much a user is willing to pay before they give up and leave.

The algorithm sees elasticity. The customer sees fairness — or exploitation.

The customer is like your wife, Ogilvy would say: you can only fool her once.

To paraphrase Oscar Wilde: the algorithm knows the price of everything, and the value of nothing.

Imagine two customers in the same store get a different price for the same tube of toothpaste because AI decided one of them is less price-sensitive. The CFO sees margin. The consumer sees a loyalty penalty.

The brand manager has to understand that price is part of identity. A premium price speaks of status, security or quality. A low price can speak of democratic access and availability. A constant discount teaches the customer that full price is only for the impatient and the uninformed. AI will sustain that dependency even more efficiently. It will calculate the ideal discount, the ideal timing, and the ideal customer. But it won't, on its own, ask whether we're turning the brand into a coupon.

In pricing, AI can: calculate price elasticity; track competitor pricing; simulate discounts and bundles; and recommend optimal price tiers.

The brand manager has to: define the value architecture; assess the impact of price on perception; protect the customer's sense of fairness; and decide when not to take every last euro a customer is willing to give.

The channel is no longer just the message. The channel is becoming the salesperson

AI knows where the customer is. The brand manager has to know where the brand belongs.

Place usually gets translated as distribution, and then handed over to the sales department — as if where something is sold has nothing to do with positioning. It has almost everything to do with it.

In 2026, Nike announced an integration with Google's AI commerce system, letting users go from discovering a product through a conversation with an AI assistant to buying it, in fewer steps.

That's the future of distribution: the store is no longer necessarily a place the customer goes to. The store shows up inside the question the customer asks. The customer won't need to open an online store and browse twenty models. They'll ask an AI agent: "Which sneakers do I need for a half-marathon if I have knee problems?"

At that point, the battle for Place will no longer be fought only on the shelf or in Google search. It will be fought inside the algorithm's recommendation.

To paraphrase McLuhan: the channel is no longer just the message. The channel is becoming the salesperson.

In distribution, AI can: predict local demand; optimise stock and delivery; recommend locations; and shorten the path from interest to purchase.

The brand manager has to: define the role of each channel; decide whether the brand should be everywhere; align availability with positioning; and understand when convenience adds value — and when it destroys desirability.

Promotion is merely the spokesperson

To paraphrase Hamlet for the AI era: to promote or not to promote is no longer the question. The question is whether you have anything worth promoting in the first place.

Cadbury's "Shah Rukh Khan – My Ad" campaign used artificial intelligence to turn the famous actor into a virtual ambassador for thousands of small local shops. One version of the ad named one shop; another named a different one. The technology wasn't decoration — it made possible an idea that classical production could never have executed at that scale. That's a good use of AI. Not because the ad was personalised. But because the personalisation was part of the meaning itself: a major star lending his fame to small shopkeepers.

The technology amplified a social and commercial idea.

By contrast, producing 5,000 versions of a banner where only the city name, the shirt colour and the product photo change isn't necessarily innovation. It's often just industrialised insignificance.

In early 2026, Starbucks introduced a multi-tier loyalty programme with personalisation and varied perks, while also using AI to predict product availability and improve operational alignment.

Here, promotion merges with product, price and distribution. Which is exactly how it should be.

To paraphrase Sun Tzu: a marketing campaign is won or lost before the first ad ever runs — in the product, the price, and the distribution.

Promotion is merely the spokesperson. It can't cure what the other three Ps have already broken.

David Ogilvy distinguished efficient advertising from effective advertising: "Efficient advertising identifies potential customers; effective advertising can create them." (Ogilvy)

AI will make marketing more efficient. The brand manager still has to make sure it's effective.

In promotion, AI can: generate large numbers of variants; personalise content and offers; optimise media buying; test headlines, visuals and formats; and identify the moment of highest purchase probability.

The brand manager has to: define one central idea; decide what's allowed to be personalised; guard tone, memory and distinctive assets; and tell a reaction apart from a business result.

Principle

The most important conclusion isn't that AI will change the 4Ps. AI will brutally expose them.

If the product isn't relevant, AI will develop its variations faster. If the price isn't credible, AI will find the limit of the customer's patience more precisely. If distribution is poor, AI will more efficiently advertise a product that isn't there. If promotion has no idea, AI will produce more formats of the same nothing.

To paraphrase Drucker: the aim of marketing isn't just to make selling superfluous. The aim is to make the brand so meaningful that promotion no longer has to repair the rest of the mix.

Artificial intelligence won't save a bad marketing mix. It will make it faster, cheaper, and more precisely measurable.

AI can help with each of the four Ps. But it can't take over the fifth P.

Principle.

And without a principle, Product becomes novelty, Price becomes a calculation, Place becomes logistics, and Promotion becomes noise.

Which is why the brand manager's main job isn't managing four letters, P. Their job is making sure all four mean the same thing.
