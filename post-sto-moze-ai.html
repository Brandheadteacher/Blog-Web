Svijet reklame — AI & marketing consulting

Što može AI, a ne može brend menadžer — i obratno?

What AI Can Do That a Brand Manager Can't — and Vice Versa

AI će napraviti tisuću oglasa prije nego što brend menadžer popije prvu jutarnju kavu. Brend menadžer mora znati zašto bi ijedan od tih oglasa trebao postojati.

AI will make a thousand ads before the brand manager finishes their first coffee of the morning. The brand manager has to know why any one of those ads should exist at all.

Branimir Zoraja 19. srpnja 2026.July 19, 2026 6 min čitanja6 min read

Kad bih prije 3 godine ChatGPT-u dao zadatak da opiše moj posao, izgledalo je to ovako: „Hrvatski kemičar i poduzetnik [...] vlasnik tvrtke Farmacia, specijalizirane za proizvodnju prirodne kozmetike [...] poznate po proizvodima poput krema Nivea Sun i O'Keeffee's za njegu kože i stopala [...] 2004. godine dobio nagradu EY Poduzetnik godine" (sic!). U jednom trenutku osobitoga nadahnuća čak bi napisao da sam pisac za djecu i mlade „čija su djela prepuna mašte, humora, avanture i pouka“.

ChatGPT se otad promijenio pa danas kaže da sam „brend strateg [...] i čuvar smisla brenda, kojega ne zanima je li ideja pametna, nego radi li posao i ostavlja li brend pametnijim nego što ga je zatekla [...]“.

Lijep napredak.

Od kemičara i dječjeg pisca do čuvara smisla brenda u samo tri godine.

Ali može li umjetna inteligencija jednoga dana zamijeniti brend menadžera?

AI ne spava. Ali ne zna zašto Coca-Cola posjeduje Božić

Coca-Cola je uz pomoć umjetne inteligencije proizvela lokalizirane verzije čuvenog božićnog oglasa, prilagodila elemente spota pojedinim gradovima i omogućila razgovor s virtualnim Djedom Božićnjakom na više od 45 jezika. U bostonskoj verziji kamion prolazi pokraj natpisa „Welcome to Boston“. U sljedećoj verziji promijenite grad, jezik, snijeg i emocionalni okidač. Ili možete rekombinirati kamione, boce, medvjede ili sâm Sundblomov lik Djeda Božićnjaka.

Brend menadžer to ne može.

Ne može u jednoj noći proizvesti 300 verzija vizuala, analizirati sto tisuća komentara, prevesti poruke na 25 jezika, izračunati vjerojatnost odustajanja kupca i zatim bez prigovora napraviti još 17 varijanti slogana.

AI ne spava. Ne traži povišicu. Ne troši vrijeme na „glupe“ sastanke niti snosi odgovornost za pogrešne odluke.

Ali AI ne zna zašto Coca-Cola uopće posjeduje Božić.

AI će napraviti tisuću oglasa prije nego što brend menadžer popije prvu jutarnju kavu. Brend menadžer mora znati zašto bi ijedan od tih oglasa trebao postojati.

Tu počinje posao brend menadžera.

Brend menadžer određuje značenje, stroj optimizira ponašanje

AI je superioran ondje gdje su potrebni brzina, količina, dosljednost i prepoznavanje uzoraka.

Može pronaći mikrosegment koji ljudi nisu primijetili. Može otkriti da kupci koji kupe jedan proizvod nakon tri tjedna češće kupuju drugi. Može usporediti tisuće recenzija, predložiti medijski plan, simulirati reakcije publike, personalizirati newsletter i proizvesti oglas u svim potrebnim formatima.

Dok brend menadžer još raspravlja je li pravilnije reći „inovativan“ ili „inventivan“, AI će već testirati obje riječi.

AI je stvarno dobar.

Toys "R" Us upotrijebio je Soru za izradu filma o mladom osnivaču Charlesu Lazarusu i nastanku njegova sna o trgovini igračkama. AI je omogućio vizualizaciju prošlosti koja nikada nije bila snimljena.

AI-sustav preporuka Starbucksa kombinira podatke o prethodnim narudžbama, dobu dana, vremenskim prilikama, lokalnim navikama i dostupnosti proizvoda kako bi svakom kupcu predložio drukčiju sljedeću kupnju.

To nisu spektakularno kreativne ideje. To je milijun malih, brzih i dosljednih odluka koje povećavaju relevantnost.

A relevantnost prodaje.

Brend menadžer određuje kakav odnos Starbucks želi graditi s kupcem. AI izračunava koju ponudu, kojem kupcu i u kojem trenutku treba pokazati.

Drugim riječima: čovjek određuje značenje, stroj optimizira ponašanje.

Najskuplja riječ u marketingu: ne

Što brend menadžer može, a AI ne može?

Brend menadžer, za početak, može reći: ne.

To zvuči skromno, gotovo birokratski. U stvarnosti je riječ o jednoj od najskupljih sposobnosti u marketingu.

Ne još jednoj ciljnoj skupini. Ne još jednom trendu na TikToku. Ne „malo modernijem logotipu“. Ne popustu koji kratkoročno podiže prodaju, a dugoročno odgaja kupce da nikada ne plate punu cijenu. Ne influenseru čija je publika velika, ali čija je vjerodostojnost mala. Ne kreativnoj ideji koju svi u sobi vole, ali je nitko na tržištu neće razumjeti. Ne novom proizvodu bez jasne uloge u portfelju. Ne širenju na kanal koji je popularan, ali strateški besmislen. Ne premium pozicioniranju bez premium iskustva. Ne još jednoj poruci koja pokušava reći sve, pa zato ne kaže ništa. Ne kampanji koja izgleda kao konkurencija. Ne promjeni identiteta samo zato što je kupcu dosadila postojeća boja. Ne metrikama koje dobro izgledaju u prezentaciji, ali ne mijenjaju ponašanje kupaca.

I, možda najvažnije: ne AI-ju kada predlaže ono što je statistički vjerojatno, ali strateški pogrešno.

Savršeno optimiziran pogrešan cilj

Da, AI može besprijekorno optimizirati zadani cilj.

Ako mu kažemo da poveća broj klikova, pronaći će publiku, poruku, format i trenutak koji će proizvesti više klikova. Ali neće nužno upozoriti da ti klikovi dolaze od pogrešnih ljudi, da su kupljeni agresivnim popustima ili da poruka dugoročno banalizira brend.

AI je izvrstan u odgovoru na pitanje:

„Kako ćemo ovo učiniti učinkovitijim?“

Brend menadžer mora prije toga postaviti neugodnije pitanje:

„Trebamo li to uopće raditi?“

Jer loše definiran cilj, savršeno optimiziran, ne postaje dobra strategija. Postaje samo brži put u pogrešnom smjeru. Možete optimizirati kratkoročnu prodaju i pritom uništiti cjenovnu poziciju. Možete povećati angažman sadržajem koji privlači pozornost, ali nema nikakve veze s identitetom brenda.

Brojke će možda rasti. Vrijednost brenda neće.

Zato brend menadžer nije osoba koja se natječe s AI-jem u brzini obrade podataka. Njegova je odgovornost odrediti što se optimizira, zašto se optimizira i koju cijenu brend ne smije platiti za bolji rezultat.

AI optimizira sredstva za promociju. Brend menadžer čuva smisao

AI će optimizirati sredstva, analizirati rezultate i predložiti najučinkovitiju raspodjelu budžeta. Brend menadžer, međutim, mora čuvati smisao brenda. On zna da rezultat koji kratkoročno izgleda najbolje ponekad dugoročno najviše šteti: ruši cijenu, slabi percepciju kvalitete ili pretvara brend u robu na akciji.

Zato njegov posao nije samo upravljanje kampanjama. Brend menadžer pregovara s prodajom koja želi promocije, financijama koje žele smanjiti troškove, upravom koja želi „nešto novo“, agencijom koja želi nagradu za dizajn i kupcima koji ne žele ništa.

AI nema hrabrost za to. Zato ne može preuzeti odgovornost.

Stroj nije izgradio prepoznatljivost promocije Heinza. Samo ju je izmjerio

U kampanji „A.I. Ketchup“ generator slika na upit „ketchup“ opetovano je stvarao oblike nalik prepoznatljivoj Heinzovoj boci. Kampanja je to predstavila kao dokaz da čak i umjetna inteligencija „misli na Heinz“ kada misli na kečap.

Duhovito? Da.

Ali strateška pouka nije da je AI kreativan. Pouka je upravo suprotna.

AI je mogao prepoznati Heinz zato što su ljudi desetljećima disciplinirano ponavljali iste distinktivne elemente: bocu, etiketu, boju, oblik i vlasništvo nad kategorijom.

Parafrazirajući Newtona, možemo reći: AI samo vidi dalje jer stoji na ramenima brendova koji su dovoljno dugo bili dosljedni.

Slab brend od AI-ja neće dobiti čudo u oglašavanju. Dobit će brže proizvedenu prosječnost.

Coca-Colin božićni oglas generiran AI-jem s početka članka ostvario je prema System 1 testiranju maksimalnu ocjenu za potencijal izgradnje brenda. To je u konačnici zgodna anegdota za objavu na LinkedInu, ali publika na LinkedInu i publika pred policom nisu uvijek ista publika.

Da parafraziramo Shakespearea u eri AI-ja: biti ili ne biti više nije pitanje marketinga. Pitanje je biti prepoznatljiv ili biti generički generiran.

AI neće zamijeniti brend menadžere. Zamijenit će njihov rutinski posao

Najopasnija rečenica u marketingu glasi:

„AI će zamijeniti brend menadžera.“

Neće.

Zamijenit će dio zadataka koje brend menadžeri neopravdano smatraju svojim profesionalnim identitetom: izradu koncepcija, analizu podataka, pisanje komercijalnih pisama i prezentacija, izradu tekstova za ambalažu i slične poslove.

AI je alat koji će, poput pojačala, u rukama dobrog brend stratega pojačati jasnoću, a u rukama lošeg marketinga buku.

Ali postoji i neugodniji nastavak.

Brend menadžer koji se zna služiti AI-jem zamijenit će brend menadžera koji se njime ne zna služiti.

Ne zato što je nužno pametniji, nego zato što će imati više vremena za ono zbog čega bi trebao biti zaposlen: prosudbu, pozicioniranje, donošenje odluka i obranu dugoročne vrijednosti brenda.

Parafrazirajući Kennedyja, mogli bismo reći: ne pitajte što AI može učiniti za vaš brend. Pitajte zna li vaš brend dovoljno jasno tko je da bi mu AI uopće mogao biti koristan.

Jer bez jasnog pozicioniranja AI neće postati kopilot brend menadžera. To je vrlo brz vozač bez odredišta. Zato budućnost pripada brend menadžerima koji će stroju prepustiti brzinu, ali nikada prosudbu.

AI je stvarno dobar. Ali brend menadžeri nisu algoritmi.

Za njih će i dalje vrijediti Bullova izreka:

"Well-managed brands live forever - only bad brand managers die."

AI-u treba delegirati: prikupljanje, uspoređivanje i strukturiranje podataka; izradu varijanti i lokalizaciju; ponavljajuće analitičke i produkcijske zadatke; simuliranje scenarija i ubrzavanje istraživanja.

Brend menadžer mora zadržati: definiranje problema i mjerila uspjeha; pozicioniranje i hijerarhiju poruka; procjenu poslovnih, reputacijskih i etičkih rizika; konačnu menadžersku prosudbu i odgovornost za poslovni rezultat.

Sljedeći članak: Umjetna inteligencija neće zamijeniti marketing. Prosječan marketing hoće →

Three years ago, if I'd asked ChatGPT to describe what I do, it would have come back with something like: "A Croatian chemist and entrepreneur [...] owner of Farmacia, a company specialising in natural cosmetics [...] known for products such as Nivea Sun and O'Keeffe's skin and foot care creams [...] won the EY Entrepreneur of the Year award in 2004" (sic!). In one particularly inspired moment, it even decided I was a children's author "whose work is full of imagination, humour, adventure and moral lessons."

ChatGPT has moved on since then. These days it says I'm "a brand strategist [...] and a guardian of brand meaning, who doesn't care whether an idea is clever, but whether it does the job and leaves the brand smarter than it found it [...]."

Nice progress.

From chemist and children's author to guardian of brand meaning — in just three years.

But could artificial intelligence one day replace the brand manager?

AI doesn't sleep. But it doesn't know why Coca-Cola owns Christmas

With the help of artificial intelligence, Coca-Cola produced localised versions of its famous Christmas ad, adapted elements of the spot to individual cities, and let people chat with a virtual Santa in more than 45 languages. In the Boston version, the truck drives past a "Welcome to Boston" sign. In the next version, you swap the city, the language, the snow, the emotional trigger. Or you recombine the trucks, the bottles, the bears, or Sundblom's own Santa Claus character.

A brand manager can't do that.

They can't produce 300 versions of a visual overnight, analyse a hundred thousand comments, translate messages into 25 languages, calculate customer churn probability, and then crank out 17 more tagline variants without complaint.

AI doesn't sleep. It doesn't ask for a raise. It doesn't waste time on "pointless" meetings, and it doesn't carry the responsibility for bad decisions.

But AI doesn't know why Coca-Cola owns Christmas in the first place.

AI will make a thousand ads before the brand manager finishes their first coffee of the morning. The brand manager has to know why any one of those ads should exist at all.

That's where the brand manager's job begins.

The brand manager determines meaning. The machine optimises behaviour

AI is superior wherever speed, volume, consistency and pattern recognition are what's needed.

It can find a micro-segment that people never noticed. It can discover that customers who buy one product are more likely to buy a second one three weeks later. It can compare thousands of reviews, propose a media plan, simulate audience reactions, personalise a newsletter, and produce an ad in every format you need.

While the brand manager is still arguing over whether "innovative" or "inventive" is the better word, AI will already be testing both.

AI really is good.

Toys "R" Us used Sora to make a film about its young founder, Charles Lazarus, and the birth of his dream of a toy store. AI made it possible to visualise a past that was never actually filmed.

Starbucks's AI recommendation system combines data on past orders, time of day, weather, local habits and product availability to suggest a different next purchase to every customer.

None of this is spectacularly creative. It's a million small, fast, consistent decisions that increase relevance.

And relevance sells.

The brand manager decides what kind of relationship Starbucks wants to build with the customer. AI calculates which offer to show which customer, and when.

In other words: the human determines meaning, the machine optimises behaviour.

The most expensive word in marketing: no

What can a brand manager do that AI can't?

For a start, a brand manager can say: no.

That sounds modest, almost bureaucratic. In reality, it's one of the most expensive capabilities in marketing.

No to yet another target group. No to the next TikTok trend. No to "a slightly more modern logo." No to a discount that lifts sales short-term while teaching customers, long-term, to never pay full price again. No to an influencer with a big audience and little credibility. No to a creative idea everyone in the room loves but nobody in the market will understand. No to a new product with no clear role in the portfolio. No to expanding into a channel that's popular but strategically pointless. No to premium positioning without a premium experience. No to yet another message that tries to say everything and therefore says nothing. No to a campaign that looks like the competition's. No to a rebrand just because the customer got bored of the current colour. No to metrics that look great in a deck but don't change customer behaviour.

And, perhaps most importantly: no to AI, when it proposes something that's statistically likely but strategically wrong.

A perfectly optimised wrong goal

Yes, AI can optimise a given goal flawlessly.

If we tell it to increase clicks, it will find the audience, message, format and moment that produce more clicks. But it won't necessarily warn you that those clicks are coming from the wrong people, that they were bought with aggressive discounts, or that the message is trivialising the brand in the long run.

AI is excellent at answering the question:

"How do we make this more efficient?"

The brand manager has to ask the more uncomfortable question first:

"Should we even be doing this?"

Because a badly defined goal, perfectly optimised, doesn't become a good strategy. It just becomes a faster route in the wrong direction. You can optimise short-term sales and destroy your pricing position while doing it. You can boost engagement with content that grabs attention but has nothing to do with the brand's identity.

The numbers might grow. Brand value won't.

So the brand manager isn't someone competing with AI on data-processing speed. Their responsibility is to decide what gets optimised, why it's being optimised, and what price the brand must never pay for a better result.

AI optimises the media budget. The brand manager guards the meaning

AI will optimise the budget, analyse the results, and propose the most efficient allocation of spend. The brand manager, however, has to guard the brand's meaning. They know that the result that looks best in the short term is sometimes the one that does the most damage long-term: it crashes the price, weakens the perception of quality, or turns the brand into a commodity on sale.

That's why their job isn't just managing campaigns. The brand manager negotiates with sales, who want more promotions; with finance, who want lower costs; with management, who want "something new"; with the agency, who wants a design award; and with customers, who don't want anything at all.

AI doesn't have the courage for any of that. Which is why it can't take on the responsibility.

The machine didn't build Heinz's recognisability. It just measured it

In the "A.I. Ketchup" campaign, an image generator prompted with "ketchup" kept producing shapes resembling Heinz's recognisable bottle. The campaign presented this as proof that even artificial intelligence "thinks of Heinz" when it thinks of ketchup.

Clever? Yes.

But the strategic lesson isn't that AI is creative. The lesson is the exact opposite.

AI could recognise Heinz because people spent decades disciplined enough to keep repeating the same distinctive assets: the bottle, the label, the colour, the shape, and ownership of the category.

To paraphrase Newton: AI only sees further because it's standing on the shoulders of brands that stayed consistent for long enough.

A weak brand won't get a miracle out of AI. It'll get mediocrity, produced faster.

The AI-generated Coca-Cola Christmas ad mentioned at the start of this article scored a maximum rating for brand-building potential in System 1 testing. Which, in the end, makes a nice anecdote for a LinkedIn post — but the audience on LinkedIn and the audience standing in front of the shelf aren't always the same audience.

To paraphrase Shakespeare for the AI era: to be or not to be is no longer the marketing question. The question is whether to be distinctive — or to be generically generated.

AI won't replace brand managers. It will replace their routine work

The most dangerous sentence in marketing is:

"AI will replace the brand manager."

It won't.

It will replace some of the tasks that brand managers have mistakenly come to treat as their professional identity: drafting concepts, analysing data, writing sales decks and presentations, writing packaging copy, and similar work.

AI is a tool that works like an amplifier: in the hands of a good brand strategist it amplifies clarity, and in the hands of bad marketing it amplifies noise.

But there's a more uncomfortable follow-on.

A brand manager who knows how to use AI will replace a brand manager who doesn't.

Not necessarily because they're smarter, but because they'll have more time for the thing they were actually hired to do: judgment, positioning, decision-making, and defending the brand's long-term value.

To paraphrase Kennedy: don't ask what AI can do for your brand. Ask whether your brand knows clearly enough who it is for AI to be of any use at all.

Because without clear positioning, AI doesn't become the brand manager's co-pilot. It becomes a very fast driver with no destination. Which is why the future belongs to brand managers who hand the machine the speed — but never the judgment.

AI really is good. But brand managers aren't algorithms.

For them, Bull's maxim still holds:

"Well-managed brands live forever - only bad brand managers die."

Delegate to AI: collecting, comparing and structuring data; producing variants and localisation; repetitive analytical and production tasks; scenario simulation and accelerating research.

The brand manager must keep: defining the problem and the measures of success; positioning and message hierarchy; assessing business, reputational and ethical risk; final managerial judgment and accountability for the business result.
